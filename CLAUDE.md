## What this project is

Atlas Panama (atlaspanama.com) is a subscription service that monitors Panama's Registro Público (public property/corporate registry) for changes to specific properties or legal entities, and notifies subscribers by email when something changes.

## Product spec: the subscriber journey

1. Potential subscriber clicks a social media/search ad (WhatsApp, FB, IG, Google, AI ad, email link, etc.) → lands on a signup form.
2. Signup form collects: name, phone number, email, and property identification. **Required: at least ONE of Finca number OR Owner name.** Código de Ubicación is always optional (most people won't know it). Also asks the subscriber's relationship to the property (owner / representative / "curious" — placeholder wording, not finalized). 
   - Rationale: the Registro Público search form supports searching by Finca alone, Owner name alone, or combinations — Finca numbers are NOT nationally unique (each province has its own Código de Ubicación catalog), so a Finca-only search can return multiple results across provinces. Owner name narrows results similarly. Someone who owns many properties under one name likely knows enough (Código de Ubicación, etc.) to narrow it down themselves.
3. Confirmation email sent to match the property, with three possible outcomes:
   - **A — exact match:** one property found → single "Confirm your property" button.
   - **B — a few matches** (e.g. same Finca across provinces): show the short list, subscriber clicks the correct one.
   - **C — more than ~15 matches:** email asks them to go back and refine their search (e.g. add Código de Ubicación or Owner name). A second failed attempt routes to a "contact customer service" fallback (email us what you know about the property). **Instrument/log every Option C occurrence** (simple spreadsheet is fine for now) — the goal is to observe where automation breaks down and reduce Option C over time. Don't try to eliminate it yet; we want the data first.
4. Confirmed match → Stripe checkout to start the subscription.
5. Subscription starts → welcome email.
6. Recurring status emails on subscriber's chosen cadence (daily/weekly/monthly): "no changes" if nothing changed. **As actually built (Sept 2026): there is no subscriber-facing cadence choice yet — every confirmed subscription runs once daily, at a fixed 19:00 America/Panama time for everyone (see `lib/dailyScheduler.js`).** This was a simplification made along the way, not a deliberate decision to drop weekly/monthly — revisit if/when subscriber demand for less-frequent updates comes up.
7. **Change notification: sent by the next day, regardless of the subscriber's chosen cadence, whenever a change is detected** — this overrides the normal schedule. Notification should describe what changed and include a before/after comparison (screenshot or extracted text) of the Prelación page.

## Change-detection logic (the core of the product)

- Monitor the **Prelación tab** of a property's record.
- Trigger a notification on: **any new line appearing, OR any change to an existing line's description/status** (e.g. "Documento Recibido" → "En Calificación" → "Trámite Agotado", or "Salida sin Registro").
- Do NOT hardcode a fixed list of status meanings to watch for — any diff between yesterday's and today's Prelación table should trigger a notification. This is intentionally broad so it catches status types we haven't seen yet.

**Real-world example of a Prelación status meaning, explained by the user (Sept 2026), from the real Ocean Drive 32B, S.A. case:** a Prelación entry showing **"Suspendido"** means that specific filing/document is what's currently being reviewed — it's the reason the *company itself* also shows as SUSPENDIDO (Ocean Drive's company-level status was suspended for having no Agente Residente for 3+ months). The company-level SUSPENDIDO status will not change until that specific Prelación entry reaches **"Trámite Agotado."** Once whatever RP is asking for (in this case, presumably naming a new Agente Residente) is answered/resolved, the review restarts and the entry's status moves again — first to something else, and if resolved correctly, eventually to Trámite Agotado — at which point the company itself should show as **VIGENTE** again, and the new Agente Residente should appear in the **Miembros Relacionados** tab. Useful general lesson: a single Prelación entry's status often explains *why* the top-level company/property status is what it is — worth cross-referencing the two when writing subscriber-facing change descriptions, not just reporting them as unrelated facts.

## Notification format — PDF-based, not Excel (Sept 2026 decision)

This changes the original assumption that individual Finca monitoring would output an Excel file. New direction:

- For an individual Finca or entity, the Registro Público site has an "Imprimir" (print) button on each tab that generates a PDF of that record.
- **The subscriber-facing output should be a PDF of the full property/entity record, not an Excel file.** No Excel involved in the subscriber-facing product at all (Excel remains relevant only for the separate, preserved "Building Search" brokerage tool).
- The "something changed" email body should include a plain-language, specific summary of what changed, covering changes across ANY tab, not just Prelación — e.g.:
  - Prelación: an entry's status changed (e.g. "Recibido" → "En Calificación" → "Trámite Agotado")
  - Datos Generales: an owner name changed from X to Y
  - (these are examples, not an exhaustive list — any detected change across any tab should be described)

- Every "something changed" email must include a legal/liability disclaimer along these lines: if the subscriber did not authorize or does not recognize the change, they should contact their attorney or accountant promptly, as it may affect their ownership rights. ## Notification channels — email is primary; SMS/WhatsApp is a later, optional addition

- Email is the required, primary channel at launch: free, supports PDF attachments, matches the deliverable described above.
- SMS/WhatsApp were discussed as a possible secondary channel for the urgent "something changed" alert specifically (not for routine no-change status updates). Tradeoffs noted: WhatsApp Business API isn't free (per-conversation billing, Meta business verification, template pre-approval for messages sent outside an active chat window), SMS has per-message costs, and neither channel is well-suited to attaching a full PDF (would need to link back to the email or a web page instead). Recommendation: build and stabilize the email flow first; consider adding an opt-in SMS/WhatsApp short-alert-with-link as a later enhancement once subscriber demand and volume justify the added cost/complexity.
